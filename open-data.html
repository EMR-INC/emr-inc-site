Open data

The argument is that this should be downloadable. So it is.

We publish the dispatch record we work from, as a plain CSV, with no account and no request form. It is the same file our own analysis reads. If we are going to say publicly funded dispatch data is being closed off, the least we can do is leave ours open.

Download the CSV What we found in it

What is in the file.

One row per dispatch event captured on the monitored talkgroups. The file is a fixed snapshot, not a live feed: it does not refresh, and the capture date below is the date it stops.

Rows

10,412 dispatch events

Columns

12

Captured

September 4, 2026. Static snapshot.

Format

CSV, UTF-8, comma separated, about 1.3 MB

Location

Street only. House numbers and coordinates are not in the file.

Source

Unencrypted public safety radio, transcribed and parsed into structured events

Licence

Use it. Attribution appreciated, not required.

The columns

id

Stable identifier for the event

dispatch_ts

When the call went out, UTC

talkgroup

The radio talkgroup it was heard on

call_type

The nature as dispatched. Free text, not a controlled vocabulary.

severity

Determinant letter, alpha through echo, where one was given

unit_raw

The unit as spoken on the air

unit_type, unit_num, unit_normalized

The same unit parsed into type and number

status

Whether the event was matched to a roster unit

incident_key

Groups the transmissions belonging to one incident

street

The street or intersection, with every number removed. 4,030 calls carried a location and 2,869 of them reduced to a street. The column is blank on the other 1,161, and on every call that carried no location at all.

What is left out, and why.

Openness is the argument, so the subtractions are listed rather than made quietly. Five things exist in our working copy and are not in this file.

Radio transcripts

The speech itself, and the model output derived from it, including patient count, breathing and consciousness status, and symptom detail. None of it is published.

Audio links

Links to the recordings. Publishing them would hand over the transcript by another route.

Member identifiers

The field tying a call to an individual member. A member's own record belongs to the member. It is not ours to publish and it is not an employer's to read.

House numbers and coordinates

The street is published. The number on it is not, and neither are the latitude and longitude, which would give the number back. A house number next to a medical nature and a timestamp describes one specific person's worst day. A street does not.

Internal identifiers

Pipeline keys and ingest timestamps that carry no information outside our own system.

Two limits worth stating plainly. This is a transcription of radio traffic, so it is what was said and heard, not an agency's official record, and it will disagree with one. And call_type is free text taken off the air, so it is uneven: the same nature appears under several spellings, and some rows carry none at all.

How the street column is built.

A dispatch address arrives as a spoken phrase, transcribed. There is no structured house-number field to drop, so the number has to be taken out of a sentence. The rule that does it runs in four stages, and it is built to fail closed: when it cannot satisfy itself that what remains is a street, it writes nothing rather than guessing.

Unit first

Apartment, unit, suite, room and floor are the most precise part of the phrase, so everything from the designator to the end of the string goes before anything else is looked at.

Then the number

The leading house number is removed, repeatedly: a transcription can carry two of them, as in 110 57 Artists Avenue. A direction of travel spoken on the air, westbound, is removed too. North and South are kept, because on these streets they are part of the name.

Then a check it is a street

What is left has to end in a word that names a kind of street, from a fixed list: street, avenue, road, drive, boulevard and the rest. If it does not, the row gets a blank. This is what rejects fragments and transcription noise, and it is the reason 1,161 located calls have no street.

Then a check for numbers

Any remaining digit disqualifies the row, with one exception: an ordinal such as 12th or 21st is kept, because it is the street's name and is shared by everyone on it. Nothing in the published column contains a house number.

One consequence is worth naming rather than leaving for someone to discover. A street is not anonymous everywhere. Of the 1,590 distinct streets in the file, 1,147 appear exactly once, and on a short street a single call with a medical nature and a timestamp narrows to very few doors. Coarsening the timestamp does not fix that, because what identifies is the street and not the clock. We publish it anyway, because the argument of this page is that a dispatch record paid for publicly should be readable, and a street without a number is what the agency's own released records contain. If you are building on this file, that is the part to handle with care.
